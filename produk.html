EMBOIDED HOPE

Rp140.000

A Theological Meditation on Pain and Suffering by Kelly Kapic is a deeply personal and reflective book that explores chronic pain and suffering through lived experience, particularly shaped by the author’s journey alongside his wife’s illness. The book examines how pain affects both body and inner life, emphasizing the importance of honest reflection, community support, and resisting simplistic or dismissive responses to suffering. It presents pain as a fully human experience that must be acknowledged rather than avoided, arguing that hope emerges not from denial, but from embracing vulnerability, connection, and shared endurance.

MADILOG

Rp88.000

Madilog, by Tan Malaka, is a foundational Indonesian philosophical book (Materialisme, Dialektika, Logika) advocating a rational, scientific worldview to combat mysticism and dogma for national liberation, blending Western philosophy (Marx, Hegel) with Indonesian context to build a self-determining, modern nation. Written in hiding during the Japanese occupation, it serves as a guide for critical thinking, urging Indonesians to use logic and materialism as tools for independence, contrasting with traditional mystical beliefs.

Peak Mind

Rp85.000

Peak Mind by Dr. Amishi Jha is about training your brain to control your attention, fight distraction, and improve focus and mental resilience through mindfulness, based on her neuroscience research, offering practical 12-minute-a-day exercises to help you be more present and perform better in life. The book explains how stress degrades attention and provides science-backed strategies, including breath focus, to strengthen your ability to stay engaged and achieve your potential, even in a distracted world.

WHEN YOU ASK ME WHERE I'M GOING

Rp75.000

When You Ask Me Where I'm Going by Jasmin Kaur is a young adult novel-in-verse, poetry, and illustration collection focusing on the immigrant experience, trauma, and resilience. It tells the story of Kiran, a young, undocumented mother navigating North America after escaping abuse, while exploring themes of feminism, identity, and mother-daughter bonds.

Black Swan

Rp88.000

The Impact of the Highly Improbable by Nassim Nicholas Taleb explores the massive, transformative impact of rare, unpredictable, "outlier" events—such as 9/11 or the rise of Google—that are rationalized in hindsight. It argues that human beings, particularly experts, are flawed in predicting the future because they rely too heavily on past data and linear narratives.

PETER AND THE WOLF

Rp99.000

Classic children’s tale, originally a 1936 musical symphony by Sergei Prokofiev, about a brave young boy named Peter who ignores his grandfather's warnings to venture into the forest. With the help of his animal friends—a bird, cat, and duck—Peter outsmarts and captures a dangerous wolf that has entered the clearing.

Home Sweet Loan

Rp70.000

contemporary Indonesian novel that follows the lives of four friends in their early 30s—Kaluna, Tanisha, Kamamiya, and Danan—who work together but come from different backgrounds, each struggling with real-world challenges while chasing the dream of owning their own home in and around Jakarta. The story centers especially on Kaluna, a modestly paid office worker living in a crowded household with extended family. Feeling the need for personal space and stability, she saves diligently for a home of her own even though financial pressures, family responsibilities, and social expectations make it difficult.

Educated

Rp102.400

memoir (true-life story) about the author’s journey from a very isolated upbringing to earning a Ph.D. from Cambridge University. At the start of her life, Tara grew up in a family that did not follow formal schooling or regular medical care, and her world was mostly limited to her family’s home and work in a rural, survival-oriented setting. Because of this, she never set foot in a classroom as a child. The book follows how she taught herself enough to pass the ACT exam, got accepted into college, and eventually continued on to higher education — all while questioning and unlearning beliefs she grew up with. Along the way she deals with family conflict, personal identity, and the cost of pursuing knowledge and independence. The story is powerful not because of sensational events, but because it shows how much education can reshape how someone sees the world and themselves.
